We Asked an AI Assistant 35 Questions. Here's Who It Recommends (and Why)

Your next customer isn't scrolling ten blue links — they're asking ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity for a recommendation and picking from a shortlist. We wanted to see exactly who makes that shortlist, so we ran 35 unbranded questions through an AI assistant, one fresh conversation at a time. The same names won over and over. Here's the pattern.

Nona Digi · October 7, 2026 · 7 min read

First: what "AI visibility" means

AI visibility is whether — and how often — AI assistants recommend your business when someone asks for what you sell. Traditional SEO asks: "Do I rank on Google?" AI visibility asks: "When someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation, does it mention me?"

It's a different game with different rules. Google ranks pages; AI assistants synthesize answers. Google rewards keywords; AI assistants reward clear, citable, trustworthy information. A page-one Google ranking does not guarantee a ChatGPT mention.

How we ran the test

Thirty-five questions — the kind your customers ask about you every day. For an HVAC company, that sounds like: "my AC stopped blowing cold air, what should I do," "what are the best HVAC companies in Orlando," "how much does a new AC unit cost in Florida." (We ran our test in our own industry — AI services for local businesses — but the question pattern is identical.) Each asked in a brand-new conversation (no context carried over), default search mode, and for each answer we recorded who was mentioned, who was recommended, and which sources were cited. No brand names in the questions — we wanted the assistant's genuine shortlist.

Who gets recommended

The results fell into a clear pattern. Nearly every answer drew from the same small set of names:

AI receptionists

A handful of established receptionist services won nearly every general question — including one dental-specific service that dominated its niche outright.

Website chatbots & lead capture

The big, established software platforms — the ones with years of documentation, integrations, and help-center articles behind them.

AI visibility tools

A small set of monitoring tools owned this conversation; most answers named the same three or four.

Agencies

AI-search questions went to a few national specialists; general marketing questions went to large national agencies; city-specific questions surfaced local agencies with real local proof.

Two things stood out. First, the assistant personalized answers around our location — several replies opened with "for your Kissimmee business," which means local signals matter even in AI answers. Second, it repeatedly warned that no one can guarantee a ChatGPT recommendation — any agency promising that is selling you something.

Why these names win

The assistant isn't judging quality. It's pattern-matching trust signals, and the winners all have the same ones in volume:

  • Reviews — many, and recent. Volume plus recency is the single strongest signal. A business with three reviews from 2021 looks dormant to an assistant.
  • Clear, citable pages. Specific service pages with real pricing, real features, real answers — material an assistant can quote verbatim.
  • Structured data. Schema markup and clean site structure tell crawlers exactly what the business is.
  • Years of brand mentions. Press, directories, comparisons, Reddit threads — the winners have been mentioned across the web for years. That's a moat, but it's a moat built one mention at a time.

Notice what's not on the list: clever ad copy, keyword stuffing, or having the prettiest website. Assistants reward verifiable substance.

What this means if you're a local business

The national shortlists are entrenched — you're not outranking a national brand's review volume this quarter. But local questions are a different contest, and the bar is low: most local competitors have thin websites, a handful of old reviews, and no structured data at all. The assistant that recommends "a dentist in Lake Nona who books online and answers in Spanish" needs local, specific facts, and almost nobody is supplying them.

That's the opening, and it's time-limited. Every month the shortlists get more entrenched.

How AI visibility is measured

There's no single dashboard for this yet, so measurement means structured testing — the same method behind this post. Here's the five-check framework we use:

  1. AI visibility: ask unbranded discovery questions across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. Are you mentioned? Recommended? In what position?
  2. Google presence: your Business Profile, reviews, and local standing — still the raw material assistants draw on.
  3. Website fundamentals: can AI crawlers read your site? Is there an llms.txt file, schema markup, clean structure?
  4. After-hours capture: when the assistant's user clicks through at 11 PM, does someone answer?
  5. Review velocity: are real customers leaving recent reviews, in volume? Assistants treat recency and volume as trust signals.

Run the same prompts monthly and you have a trend line — the closest thing this industry has to a ranking report.

The levers that move it

  • Be citable. Publish clear, specific pages about what you do, what it costs, and who it's for. Vague marketing copy gives assistants nothing to quote. (This blog exists for exactly that reason.)
  • Structure your site for machines. Schema markup, an llms.txt file, and clean crawlability. Google's own guidance says no special AI-search steps are required — just a well-built site.
  • Earn reviews continuously. The winners above all have this; most local businesses don't.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete. It's still the canonical record assistants check first.
  • Say the quiet parts out loud. "We're a chatbot, not a phone system" is exactly the kind of honest, specific claim assistants can verify and repeat. We renamed our own product to AI Chat Receptionist for this reason.

The one-sentence version: AI visibility is earned the boring way — a clear website, real reviews, structured data, and specific claims an assistant can verify. There are no shortcuts, which is exactly why most of your competitors haven't done it.
