For Christmas light installers: the short season is won in October

Most trades get twelve months to find their customers. You get about eight weeks. That changes everything about how the work is won: the enquiries that matter arrive in a rush, and they go to whoever answers first and clearest. October — this month, right now — is when that gets decided.

Nona Digi · October 14, 2026 · 6 min read

An eight-week season forgives nothing

If a plumber has a quiet month, the year can still recover. If a Christmas light installer has a quiet November, the season is simply gone — the calendar doesn't offer a second one. That pressure runs both ways: homeowners deciding on lights are working to a deadline too. Nobody comparison-shops for three weeks over a roofline. They enquire with two or three companies, and the first clear, confident reply usually takes the job.

So the real contest isn't December, and it isn't really about who installs the prettiest display. It's about two things that both happen before the ladder comes out: whether people find you, and whether anyone answers when they reach out. October is the month to get both right.

Getting found: a website that answers the real questions

When a homeowner starts looking, their questions are remarkably consistent — and they're the same questions people now type into AI assistants, which is why answering them plainly on your website does double duty. This is the practical side of answer engine optimization and generative engine optimization: be the source the assistant can quote. The questions to answer, in the customer's own words:

  • "Do you design it, or do I pick?" Explain how design works — whether you bring ideas, work from the homeowner's, or both.
  • "What's included?" Say clearly what the service covers: the lights themselves, installation, timers, extension runs, and what happens if a bulb fails mid-season.
  • "What happens in January?" Takedown is half the peace of mind you're selling. Explain how removal and storage work — whether you store the lights or hand them back, and how next year gets easier.
  • "Roughly what does it cost?" You don't need to publish a price list. But explain honestly what shapes the price — the length of the roofline, a second story, trees and pathways, renting versus buying the lights — and a homeowner can place themselves. Silence on cost doesn't protect you; it just sends them to the company that explained it.
  • "How early do I need to book?" If your calendar fills, say so, and say when. A clear booking window creates urgency that's actually true.

Add real photos of your own installs, the neighborhoods or towns you cover, and last season's reviews answered and visible. That's the material a homeowner — and an assistant recommending one — needs to pick you with confidence.

Getting answered: replies that land while you're on a ladder

Here's the structural problem of the season: the hours when enquiries pour in are exactly the hours you're on a roof. Homeowners write in the evening, after dinner, when they're picturing the house lit up. You're finishing a job, driving home, or already booked solid the next day. The enquiry waits until morning — and by morning they've heard back from someone else.

In an eight-week season, a slow reply isn't a small leak. It's the whole business, dripping. That's why we'd put an AI chat receptionist on the website for the season: it answers the moment someone writes in, explains your design, installation, takedown, and storage the way your own pages describe them, and captures the name, address, and phone number while the homeowner is still excited. You come down off the ladder to a list of real enquiries instead of a silent inbox.

Don't forget last year's customers

Your warmest enquiries this season won't come from strangers. They'll come from the people whose houses you lit last year, and from their neighbors, who watched the whole street admire the result. A short, friendly note in October — we're booking now, your spot from last year is held until a date you name — reaches people who already trust your work and need no convincing. And when they reply in the evening, the same rule applies: the company that answers warmly and quickly keeps the customer. Loyalty, in a short season, still goes to whoever responds.

Your October, in order

  1. One page that answers the five questions above, in your own words, with your real photos.
  2. Your service area and booking window stated plainly — where you work, and when your calendar realistically fills.
  3. Last season's reviews replied to, and a standing habit of asking each happy customer this season, at the moment the lights first switch on.
  4. An evening test: message your own company at 8 PM like a customer would, and read what comes back. If the honest answer is "nothing until morning," fix that before November does it for you.

Do those four things this month and the season changes character. You're no longer chasing enquiries between rooftops — you're choosing from the ones that arrived while you worked, from homeowners who already understood what you offer and what shapes the price.

The one-sentence version: in an eight-week season, the job goes to the company that's easy to find and quick to answer — and both of those are decided in October.
